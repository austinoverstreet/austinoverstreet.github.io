# That’s Life

Growing up, much like all kids, I just wanted to become an adult. I wanted to get there as fast I could. I wanted the freedom to do whatever, the ability to run my own life, and even a wife and kids to match. Little did I know that there was a lot of other bullshit that came along with being an adult. As a kid, you don’t see or hear about taxes. Sure, we talk about them a bit here and there. But let's be honest, no one care about them till they see have their paycheck was  that T word. Only then does anyone start to care.

I find it to be funny that we all want to grow up, then when it happens, we just want to go back. It reminds me of how Andy says "I wish there was a way to know you're in the good old days before you've actually left them" in The Office.

I’m at a weird point in my life. Twenty-eight, married, with three cats. I feel like I’ve got it made.

Then, I see that someone from my high school passed. Someone I haven’t talked to in years. Someone I’ve known since we were so young. That when it hits me that life has run away with me. I think that everyone does this in some shape or form. We lose touch with people. Not always because of bad blood. Maybe you move away. Maybe you just stop talking. It happens. It’s all part of life.

Then you see that they’re gone, and you think about where all the time went. You remember when you thought about reaching out. Saying hey, being a friend, but you didn’t. You got busy and forgot to hit send, you figured they didn’t want to talk to you, didn’t want to bother them.

Now you can’t.

## What do you do?

Well, it’s not easy.  Let’s start by saying that.

Losing someone is tragic. Just because you hadn’t talked to them, or you’ve fallen out of touch doesn’t mean you can’t feel some type of way about it. You can and it means something that you do. It means you cared about that person. Maybe you hadn’t talked in a while, at one point, you cared about them.

That’s enough.

It means they had an impact on your life in some way. I think that gives meaning to their life. Give meaning to their memory that you now hold with you. Now, you have to do something with that memory. You can either hold onto it or share it with others. For me, I choose to share it because when people pass, they’re not really gone. We hold them in memories, giving them life whenever we think about them. Once the last people who remembered them is gone, then they are truly gone.

I wrote a letter about a friend that recently passed away. Well, I wrote him a letter. I’ve been debating on sending it to him. Knowing that he wouldn’t read it but one of his family members might. Wondering if it would be nice to share my memories of him. Furthermore, I haven’t finished the letter or decided what to do with it. I’m thinking of posting it here, for the world to be able to see.

Then, even after I’m gone. His memory will live on.
