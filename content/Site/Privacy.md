_Last updated: October 2026_

This is a small author site, and I’d like to keep it that way: I don’t track you. Here’s the plain-language version of what happens when you visit.

## What I collect

**Your email address, only if you subscribe.** When you sign up for the Chaos Theory newsletter, the address you type goes to [Kit](https://kit.com), the service I use to send it. Kit stores it and emails you a confirmation link. You are only added to the list once you click that link, and every email I send has an unsubscribe link at the bottom. If you unsubscribe, you stop receiving emails. Kit’s own [privacy policy](https://kit.com/privacy) covers how they handle the data.

**Messages you send me.** If you use the contact form, your name, email, and message go to [FormSubmit](https://formsubmit.co), a form service that emails them to me and may keep a copy for up to 30 days. If you email me directly, I see your address and what you wrote. Either way, I only use it to reply.

## What I don’t do

- **No analytics and no tracking.** I don’t use Google Analytics, Facebook pixels, or any similar tool.
- **No advertising** and no selling or sharing of your information.
- **No cookies** for tracking. The site stores one small note in your browser: whether you prefer the dark or light look (and, if you choose, whether to play the background video). That stays on your device.
- **No outside fonts.** The fonts are hosted on this site, so your browser doesn’t contact Google to load them.

## Who else sees something

- **FormSubmit delivers contact-form messages.** See [FormSubmit’s privacy policy](https://formsubmit.co/privacy.pdf).
- **GitHub Pages hosts this site.** Like any web host, GitHub may keep ordinary server logs, such as your IP address, when you load a page. See [GitHub’s privacy statement](https://docs.github.com/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement).
- **Links to other sites.** Buy buttons go to Amazon, and the social links go to Instagram, TikTok, and Royal Road. Once you click through, their privacy rules apply, not mine.
